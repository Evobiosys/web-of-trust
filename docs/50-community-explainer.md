# 50 — What is this? (for the community)

## The short version

You meet someone at a dance. Maybe you dance together, maybe you just talk after. You both tap your phones together for twenty seconds. Now you're in each other's web.

That's it. That's the whole thing.

The more real relationships you weave this way, the more of the world opens up around you — gatherings you'd never have heard about, things you can borrow, people worth meeting. Nothing about you gets sold. Nothing gets watched. Nothing gets mined for ads.

This is a prototype. It's being built with the community, not handed down to it. Nothing here is finished, and nothing here is trying to be big. It's trying to be honest.

## Why meet in person?

Online, anyone can pretend to be anyone. A profile picture proves nothing. A message could be from anyone.

On the dance floor, you know who you met. You saw their face. You heard their voice. You felt whether the meeting was real.

That's why this whole thing runs on in-person meetings, not on messages sent from a distance. Trust that starts in a body, in a room, with another person in front of you — that's trust you can actually rely on. Everything else follows from that one rule.

## The three closenesses

When you meet someone and tap phones, you choose together how close you are — right then, in the moment.

**Contact.** You just met. You hold each other's basic info — enough to find each other again.

**Friend.** You're in each other's web now. You see what your friends choose to share: their gatherings, their offers, the people they vouch for.

**Close friend.** The inner room. The people you'd trust with more — the harder conversations, the smaller circles, the things not meant for everyone.

You can grow a Contact into a Friend later, when it's true. Nothing is locked in at first meeting.

## What hosts control

Whoever hosts a gathering decides who can even see it exists. The choices are simple: everyone, anyone connected to the community, friends only, or close friends only.

Here's the part that matters: a private gathering isn't shown to outsiders as locked, behind some "request access" button. It simply doesn't appear. There's nothing to feel left out of, because there's nothing to see.

No FOMO by design. What you don't have access to, you don't even know is happening.

## Borrowing and lending

Speakers. A DJ table. Cacao. A venue for the night. The things a dance community actually needs, sitting unused in people's closets and garages.

Your web can ask to borrow what you offer. You can ask to borrow from them.

When something comes back, both people answer one simple question: "Do you feel complete?" Nothing gets counted. Nothing gets ranked. Just whether it felt right — held between you and the people closest to you, not broadcast to anyone else.

## Your data lives with you

Your people, your gatherings, your web — all of it lives on your phone, not on a company's computers. There's no account to close, because there's no account to begin with.

If you leave, you take everything with you. Nothing stays behind on someone else's computer, owned by someone else, sold to someone else.

Seeing who your friends know only happens with each person's consent. And if someone can see you through your web, you can see that they can. No one watches you that you can't also see watching.

## Always free

The core of this will always be free. The dance floor is a commons — it doesn't belong to anyone, and neither should this.

## Where this is going

This is a prototype. Some of it will change. Some of it might get thrown out entirely. The community shapes what this becomes — not the other way around.

If something feels off, say so. If something feels missing, say that too. Doubts are welcome here. This only works if it's built with the people who'll actually use it, on the actual dance floor, meeting each other face to face.
