Web of Trust: the ecosystem

What exists, what only looks like it exists, and the five decisions that are actually blocking. Focused on one case: information about apartments in cities.

Assembled 2026-09-01 from the code on disk, not from the plans. Every module named here was opened and read.

The finding that reorders everything

Most of the ecosystem already exists. It is in the other repo.

rebiosys on branch query-infra is not an early draft of last night's browser demo. It is a deeper system that the demo is a thin slice of. Neither is a superset of the other, so building forward on either means porting from the other. That decision has not been made and it blocks everything downstream.

And one schema already encodes the economics. inventory-store/src/types.ts defines these categories:

housing · lendable-want-back · give-without-worry · network-introduction · community-hostable

give-without-worry is the "I failed to sell it and now it can leave passively" intuition, already modelled, already distinct from lending something you want returned. Beside it sit care_if_lost: high|medium|none, which is the honest version of a deposit, and source: transcript-extraction, which means the schema was written expecting records to arrive from parsed conversation rather than a form.

The apartment spine, corrected

Cold start for apartments is not slow. It is structurally unreachable.

The anonymity floor exists so that what gets offered cannot be traced back to one person. Done correctly it counts distinct authors. Then one holder would need seven different neighbours who each pre-announced a flat inside her own chat export, before a single query returns anything. A 100 to 200 member Grätzl group plausibly yields a handful of such messages from one to six distinct people across its whole retained window, and most decay before anyone asks.

The flagship template and the flagship safety property are mutually exclusive. Lowering k is a retreat, not a fix.

The way out is to invert the direction of the data.

Declared supply plus standing demand needs one person on each side, not seven. Someone who is giving up a flat declares it; someone who is looking registers a standing desire; they meet. That is push, not pull, and it has no k problem, no third-party consent problem, and no dependence on anyone's message history.

Which lands exactly on the "collect desires" idea. It is not an adjacent feature. It is plausibly the product.

And the motive is not altruism. It is self-interest, which is much sturdier.

The framing to drop: "why would someone give away a valuable lead?" People see through altruism framing and it does not survive contact with a scarce market.

The framing that holds, for Vienna specifically: an outgoing tenant has real skin in finding a successor. Kaution (deposit) return, Ablöse recovery, a clean Übergabe, and not being processed through a landlord's applicant casting. A Nachmieter from a trusted network is worth more to the leaver than to the seeker. That is the strongest-grounded motive found in the whole research pass.

Corrected today. The demo's floor counted messages, not people. Seven messages from one neighbour cleared a floor of seven and every one was hers. It now counts distinct authors within the relevance band, with six tests pinning it, including the exact case that used to pass wrongly.

Still open in rebiosys. The k floor there gates aggregates; the reveal_identity path is a gate-2 decision, not a k decision, so a single holder matching a single query is not k-gated today. Whether it should be decides whether a desires registry is buildable as specified.

The overlooked mechanism. network-access/contracts.ts already supports per-pair signed k overrides. Two people who know each other can agree a lower floor between themselves. That may be the real answer to cold start: not lowering k globally, but letting specific pairs agree.

What does not exist that looks like it does

The desire registry. QuestHub's quests are one-line descriptions with no structured registry. The thing you described, standing records of what people want with matching semantics and thresholds, does not exist anywhere in your work yet. Given the argument above that it is probably the product, this is the most consequential gap on the page.

Complete Memory's ingestion pipeline is stalled and its main corpus is confirmed absent from disk. Grapevine maps social-media relationships, not resource sharing. The maps and world-regions work is at continental zoom, not building or neighbourhood.

Two contradictions between the transcripts and the code

1. A central RAG would dissolve the privacy claim.

One transcript considers "central RAG compilation and jurisdiction-specific hosting". Everything built assumes the opposite: the vault is on the owner's device, in browser IndexedDB or a local JSONL daemon, and the byte-identical answer is meaningful precisely because nothing central ever sees the data. A central index is not a deployment detail, it is a different product with a different promise. Decide it before anything else is built on the current foundation.

2. The system protects the holder, not the asker.

types.ts:131 puts from: Identity in the query envelope and main.ts:452 renders the asker's name to the person being asked. The guarantee is one-directional by construction.

For apartments that is fine, since looking for a flat is not shameful. But the high-trust situations that matter most, separation, job loss, illness, financial trouble, leaving an abusive relationship, invert it: there the need itself is the stigmatising thing and the asker is the vulnerable party. Serving those requires an ask that is anonymous to the holder while still accountable to the network, which is a different mechanism and a harder one.

Two more from the transcripts with no counterpart in the build. Anti-exfiltration and anti-distillation are named as requirements and have no implementation; they are the same problem as the aggregate leak across many queries over time, which is research rather than engineering. And the exit strategy, interoperability and forkability including "selling part of the business while keeping the network open", is a commitment that nothing in the architecture currently honours or blocks.

Supply comes from transitions, not from generosity

The supply side for apartments is people subletting or moving out early.

They are not being generous. They are solving their own problem: Kaution back, Ablöse recovered, a clean Übergabe, and not being processed through a landlord's applicant casting. A Nachmieter from a trusted network is worth more to the leaver than to the seeker.

That generalises. A life transition is a burst of demand and a burst of supply at the same time, it is time-boxed, which gives every record a natural expiry and solves staleness, and it is exactly what people do not post publicly. Moving out generates a whole bundle at once: the flat, the furniture that cannot be moved, the appliances, the plants, the parking spot, the Genossenschaft position, and the neighbourhood know-how.

The pattern: supply comes from holders, and the deepest need comes from askers.

Every situation where the system already works is one where the exposed person is the one holding something. Every situation where the need is most acute is one where the exposed person is the one asking. The design protects exactly the half that is easier to protect.

Leaving an abusive relationship is where this stops being an abstraction. A named ask, delivered to a network that may overlap with the person being left, is not a missing feature. It is a hazard. That case is deliberately held back until anonymous asking exists and has been attacked on a lower-stakes case first.

The three to build after apartments

1 · Verlassenschaft

Needs nothing architecturally. What it needs is a referral channel into funeral homes, Notare and the Entrümpelung firms that currently charge for this.

2 · Befristung ending

Needs lease-end-date targeting, not new privacy technology. The volume is large and the timing is knowable in advance, which is what a registry wants.

3 · Care equipment

Chosen because it is harder. Rotes Kreuz already lends this equipment cheaply, so access is not the bottleneck. Disclosure is. That makes it the cleanest real-world test of whether protecting the asker is worth anything.

The cheapest first step toward anonymous asking, if it is wanted: suppress the asker's name at main.ts:452 and 483 for templates flagged sensitive, while keeping the underlying identity intact for accountability. That is a small, reversible change and it is enough to pilot the question before anything zero-knowledge is attempted. The full version is a graph-membership proof, showing "inside your network within N hops" without naming who, plus an accountability record openable only through an abuse process, plus batched delivery so a request cannot be correlated with an event.

What it costs, honestly: fishing, harassment, an untraceable bad actor, and the loss of the social pressure that a recognised name provides. That pressure is a large part of why anyone answers at all.

The decisions, in order

  1. Local-first or central RAG. These are two different products. Everything built assumes the first.
  2. Does the asker need protecting too? One-directional serves apartments. The situations worth serving after apartments invert the vulnerability.
  3. Measure before deciding the apartment focus. Export five real Vienna groups, run the existing matcher over all five templates, count hits, distinct authors, and human-rated actionable leads per month. Hours of work. Do not tune k before this exists.
  4. Which codebase is the trunk? Browser demo or rebiosys daemon. Everything downstream waits on this.
  5. Does the k floor cover single-holder matches, or only aggregates? This decides whether the desires registry is buildable as specified.
  6. Third-party consent. A shared item relays a non-user's verbatim words. They never consented and cannot revoke. The household exemption thins once this is org-operated with a hosted mediator. Needs an Austrian DSB or lawyer opinion before any pilot.
  7. The seven trust-sharing decisions from the scenario map, still unanswered since 2026-08-30.

One boundary condition the ecosystem plan crosses. The demo's deterministic encryption nonce is safe only while the pair key is a two-party shared secret. A mediator and a Matrix transport are the multi-party case. Adding either means the nonce must become random and the byte-identity test must switch to an injected one.
